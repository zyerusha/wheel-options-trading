Capital deployed

Stacked — each day's bands sit on top of each other; the stack's height is your total committed capital (the grey line, in dollars):

  • Put collateral = strike × 100 × contracts
  • Assigned shares carry their cost basis
  • A covered call adds nothing — the capital is already in the shares

Cumulative premium & realized P/L

Premium is credit taken in at open. Realized P/L nets buy-backs, expirations and stock called away — it is what actually stuck.

Puts or calls: where the wheel is right now

Every active cycle's current capital, split by component — a cycle holding shares from one assignment while separately running a fresh cash-secured put contributes to both buckets below, not just one:

  • Cash-Secured Puts — cash securing a short put, waiting to be assigned or expire
  • Covered Calls — shares backing a short call currently written against them, waiting to be called away
  • Holding Shares — shares held with no call currently written; capital not generating premium right now
  • Hedged / Other — protective puts, long calls, and netted credit-spread collateral — capital that isn't plain put or stock exposure

Weighted toward Covered Calls/Holding = most capital tied up in stock. Weighted toward Cash-Secured Puts = mostly waiting on assignment decisions. Zero in a bucket is a real reading, not a gap — it means nothing currently active is in that phase.

Monthly cash flow

Net cash flow = Realized credits - Realized debits - Fees, booked the day cash actually settles:

  • Credits: premium sold, dividends
  • Debits: premium paid to close a short or open a hedge
  • Independent of expiration date or whether the position is still open
  • Share assignment costs and call-away proceeds are capital shifts, not income — excluded (see Capital deployed above)

Cash flow vs. wheel P/L gap

Cash flow books a credit the moment premium is sold; wheel P/L only books it once a position actually closes. The line is the running total of that difference:

  • Rising — collecting more premium than you're realizing, usually because open positions are building up (normal for a growing wheel, worth knowing)
  • Flattening or falling — closes are catching up to, or outpacing, new premium sold
  • Climbing week after week with no pullback is the thing to watch — can mean premium sitting in open risk, not banked profit

Wheel PPD by week

Profit Per Day = realized option P/L (never stock P/L) ÷ calendar days. The line is the wheel's PPD to date — its right edge is your PPD right now, and it tracks toward the headline figure above. The bars are each week's own realized P/L ÷ 7, spiky because P/L lands in lumps when positions close.

Annualized Wheel ROC

Option P/L only (never stock P/L) ÷ time-weighted average capital, scaled to a year. Assigned shares count as capital by their cost basis, not by their price move. Tickers marked ~ include a strike-based proxy for stock held before this export.

Four percentage columns, each answering a different question — hover any cell for its formula:

  • ROI — net P/L (incl. stock) ÷ time-weighted avg collateral
  • Wheel ROC — option P/L only ÷ time-weighted avg collateral
  • Net Option Yield — option P/L only ÷ initial (day-one) collateral
  • Total Position ROI — option + stock P/L (realized & unrealized) + dividends ÷ initial collateral

None is "the" right answer — different lenses on the same cycle.
